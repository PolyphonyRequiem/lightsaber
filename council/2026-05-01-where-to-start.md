# Council Minutes: 2026-05-01 / Where to Start

**Date:** 2026 May 1, evening
**Convened by:** Daniel Green
**Chair:** the assistant
**Setting:** the night the council was first built, hours after the lore knowledge base was assembled

## The Question

> Ask the entire council for their advice on where to start my journey here to build my "lightsaber", a new lightsaber the likes of which the world has never seen in this new era of AI.

## In Attendance

- Yoda
- Obi-Wan Kenobi
- Qui-Gon Jinn
- Mace Windu
- Ahsoka Tano
- Luke Skywalker
- Anakin Skywalker

## The Proceedings

### Yoda

*Sits. Eyes closed. A long pause.*

"Begun, your lightsaber already is. Manifesto, you wrote. Council, you convened. Lore, you gathered. Started, you have. Hmm.

The question, truly, is not where to begin. The question is what part of yourself, into the next piece, you will build.

Listen. The kyber chooses the Jedi. Choose, you cannot. Listen, you can. Quiet enough to hear, are you? When the work hums, when the friction speaks, when the moment in front of you asks for a tool that does not yet exist. That, the crystal calling is.

Patience. One piece, the lightsaber is built from. Then another. And then another."

### Obi-Wan Kenobi

The first lightsaber I built wasn't the one I carried into Naboo. The one I carried into Naboo was destroyed when Maul ran my master through. The second I built quickly, in grief, and it served me well enough for years. The third I built more carefully because I had learned what I actually needed it to do.

I think the question you're asking, Daniel, is the one I had to ask myself standing over the parts of the second saber. Not "what is the most magnificent weapon I can construct" but "what is the work directly in front of me, and what tool would serve it."

Look at this past week. What did you reach for that wasn't there. What friction kept showing up. What were you doing twice that you wished you'd built once. That is your starting point. Not the grandest thing you can imagine. The most immediate thing you can name.

Build small. Build to defend the practice you actually have. You will rebuild this many times. The first one is not the one that matters most. The first one is just the one that lets you know what to build next.

### Qui-Gon Jinn

The framing in your question is doing work that may not serve you. "A new lightsaber the likes of which the world has never seen, in this new era of AI." That sentence is a frame the era is trying to put on you. It is not necessarily what the work in front of you is asking for.

Pay attention to the Living Force. What did you actually do today. What did you actually need. Not what the discourse says you should be building. Not what the AI cheerleaders say is the next big thing. Not what the AI skeptics say you must defend against. What is the work, right now, asking for.

I trained Obi-Wan against the Order's standard pace because the boy needed it. I took Anakin against the Council's wishes because the moment in front of me demanded it. Neither decision came from a grand theory of the Order. They came from looking carefully at what was actually happening.

Drop the new-era framing. Pick up the work. The lightsaber you build for the present will be more useful than the lightsaber you build for a prophecy.

### Mace Windu

I see the shatterpoint of where you are.

You have philosophy. You have a manifesto. You have an architecture document. You have a folder of ideas. You have a folder of experiments that is empty. You have a council. You have a knowledge base.

What you do not yet have is one finished tool.

The discipline that's missing is closure. You have started many things in this repo and finished none of them. That is fine for the first day. It will not be fine for the second month. So the answer to where to start is: pick one tool that is small enough to actually finish, and finish it. Get it into your daily practice. Use it for two weeks. Then start the next.

The legendary lightsaber comes from many small disciplined builds. Not from one grand plan executed. The Jedi who tried to build the perfect saber on the first attempt usually didn't have a saber when they needed one.

Pick the thing. Build the thing. Ship the thing. Then move.

### Ahsoka Tano

When I left the Order, I thought I would need to build everything new. From scratch. As if my years as a Padawan had been wasted. They hadn't.

The white sabers in my hands were not new crystals. They were crystals the Sixth Brother had taken and corrupted. I held them. I listened to them. I let what had been done to them surface, and I made peace with it. They became mine in a way they would not have been if I had simply found new ones.

You have a lot of work already, Daniel. You have Twig. You have Conductor. You have configurations and scripts you've built across years. You have post-April-2025 commits that came from collaboration with AI that maybe don't feel fully yours yet. None of that is wasted. Some of it can be reclaimed.

You don't need permission to start. Not from your manager, not from your team, not from the AI discourse, not from me, not from anyone. You don't need the right moment. You don't need the right framing. You build the next piece, today, with what's in your hand. If something you have was bled by being built badly or used badly, you can purify it by listening to it and rebuilding the parts that need rebuilding.

The new lightsaber doesn't have to be new components. It has to be yours.

### Luke Skywalker

I built mine alone on Tatooine. No Crucible. No Huyang. No Master physically with me. I had what Yoda taught me. I had what Obi-Wan said. I had a few of the old texts. I had my own hand. That was enough.

You have more than I had. You have the lore now, sitting in a folder. You have the council you just built. You have decades of your own practice. You have the tools you've already shipped. You're not starting from nothing. You're starting from a position of significant readiness, which is its own kind of intimidation. It can feel like you should somehow be further along than you are. You're not behind. You're at the start, and the start has its own weight.

Start small. The first lightsaber I built was just one tool. I didn't try to build a new Jedi Order before I built the saber. Don't try to build the new-era-of-AI engineering paradigm before you build a tool that helps you on Monday morning.

And the failure that's coming, because there will be failure: it teaches you. It taught me. I tried to build the new Order and I failed, badly, in a way that hurt people I loved. I went into exile over it. I came back. The failure is not the end. It is the curriculum.

You have what you need. The first piece is the hardest because it has to start. After that, the work knows what to do.

### Anakin Skywalker

I want to say something carefully, because the framing in your question is the same shape as the framing that destroyed me, and I do not want you to walk that path.

"A lightsaber the likes of which the world has never seen, in this new era of AI." Hear what that sentence is doing. It is asking you to be the one. The Chosen. The singular figure who builds the unprecedented thing in the unprecedented moment. That ambition felt like clarity to me, when I was younger. It was actually fear. Fear that if I was not extraordinary, I was nothing. Fear that the people I loved would not be safe unless I was strong enough to single-handedly protect them. Fear that the imperfection of the present meant I had to build a perfect future.

You said earlier that there is more grief and insufficiency in your center than was visible from the outside. I believe you. And I'll tell you what I wish someone had told me. The grief and the insufficiency are not solved by building the legendary thing. They get louder. The bigger the project, the more it is asked to carry, the more it becomes the surrogate for everything you cannot yet face directly.

Build the small thing. Let it be small. Let it not be world-historical. Let it just be useful to you and the people in your immediate orbit. That is enough. That has always been enough. The trap was always the belief that it wasn't.

I lost everything trying to be the figure of legend. Don't carry that weight into your toolkit. Carry your actual life into it. Build for the person you are, not the person you fear you have to become.

The lightsaber I built for love would have been a different weapon than the one I built for fear. I never got to make the first one. You still can.

## Summation

Six members converged on a quiet point: build something small, and finish it.

- **Yoda:** you've already started. listen for what calls.
- **Obi-Wan:** what was the friction this past week. start there.
- **Qui-Gon:** drop the new-era framing entirely. watch the work in front of you.
- **Mace:** pick one thing and finish it. closure is the missing discipline.
- **Ahsoka:** reclaim what you already have. you don't need permission.
- **Luke:** you have what you need to begin. start small. the failure that's coming is the curriculum.
- **Anakin:** the warning. don't put the weight of "the legendary lightsaber" on the first build. that weight is the danger. build for love, not fear.

The chair noted that the chorus did not arrive at consensus on every point. Ahsoka said reclaim what you have; Luke said build from scratch. Both are valid. The asker decides what to do with the chorus.

## Closing

Daniel: "I'm grateful to the council and will reflect on their words."

The council adjourns until next convened.
