## The Hook

Every engineer's toolkit starts somewhere. A `git init`. An empty README. A folder that doesn't know what it's for yet. The first commit is always small, always quiet, always the most important one in the history.

There's something worth writing about in the moment a personal toolkit becomes a *project*. The shift from "I should organize my dotfiles" to "this is a thing I'm building". The act of naming it. The act of giving it a manifesto before it has any code.

## What the Post Could Cover

- The case for building your own tools in the AI era (the lightsaber thesis, expanded).
- The taxonomy of personal infrastructure: configs, scripts, integrations, standards, experiments, ideas. Why those categories, why those names.
- The tension between metaphor and convention. When does naming a folder `kyber-crystal/` help, when does it just get in the way?
- The role of AI in personal toolkit construction. Not as a replacement for the craft, but as a force multiplier on the parts you already understand.
- The first commit. Why it matters more than any other.

## Why It Could Be Good

Most "build your own tools" content is either dotfile-porn or productivity-influencer fluff. There's an opening for a piece that takes the philosophy seriously without taking itself too seriously. The Star Wars / cyberpunk lens of the lightsaber README is a frame that hasn't been overdone.
