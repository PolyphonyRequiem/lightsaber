# Mentorship and Lineage

The Jedi Order is, at its operational core, a teaching institution. Doctrine, technique, and philosophy travel from one mind to another through a structured but intimate apprenticeship: a single Master, a single Padawan, a Force-bond between them, and the slow transmission of a lifetime of practice. The same is true, in mirror image, of the Sith. When that chain is broken, what survives is fragmentary, scavenged, and reconstructed by whoever is left standing.

This document covers the Master-Padawan relationship, the formal training path, the canonical lineages worth tracing, the institutions that preserved knowledge, and what happened to mentorship after the Order fell.

## The Master-Padawan Relationship

### Selection

The traditional Jedi path begins long before a Master is involved. Force-sensitive infants are brought to the Coruscant Temple and raised in clans (Bear, Katarn, Heliost, and others), supervised by senior Jedi such as Yoda. They train together as Initiates until roughly thirteen, at which point a Knight or Master must select one as their Padawan. An Initiate who reaches the upper age limit without being chosen is reassigned to one of the Jedi Service Corps (Agricultural, Medical, Educational, Exploration).

Selection is usually the Master's call, but Padawans have agency at the margins. In *The Phantom Menace*, Anakin Skywalker is brought into the Order at Qui-Gon Jinn's insistence over the Council's objection; after Qui-Gon's death, Obi-Wan Kenobi takes Anakin as a deathbed promise rather than a measured pairing. Ahsoka Tano's assignment to Anakin in *The Clone Wars* is the inverse: Yoda hands her down as a deliberate corrective, pairing the impulsive Knight with a student who would force him to teach.

### The Bond

Every working Master-Padawan pair forms what canon material calls a training bond, a Force connection built through proximity, trust, and shared danger. It is a real link in the Force: the partners can sense each other's emotional state, locate each other across distance, and in moments of crisis communicate impressions or warnings. *The Clone Wars* and the *Ahsoka* novel by E. K. Johnston both depict Anakin and Ahsoka feeling each other across a battlefield. In *Ahsoka* (Disney+), Sabine Wren's bond with Ahsoka manifests as a literal Force-projection guidance during her training on Peridea.

A training bond is not the same as the rarer phenomenon called a dyad in the Force, the once-a-generation pairing seen between Rey and Ben Solo in the sequel trilogy. The training bond is built; the dyad is found.

### Daily Life

Training is not a curriculum, it is a way of living together. A Padawan accompanies their Master on assignments, fights at their side, eats and meditates with them, and is expected to absorb judgment by exposure as much as by lesson. The braid worn over the right shoulder marks the Padawan's status until Knighthood, when it is ceremonially cut.

Within the Temple, the rhythm includes lightsaber katas in the training salles, meditation in the Room of a Thousand Fountains, scholarly study in the Archives under the Chief Librarian (Jocasta Nu in the prequel era), and sparring with both peers and instructor droids. In the field, training continues by improvisation: Obi-Wan teaches Anakin during running gun-battles on Geonosis, Coruscant, and Tatooine; Anakin teaches Ahsoka during the Battle of Christophsis and the rescue on Teth in the *Clone Wars* feature.

### "This Weapon Is Your Life"

The doctrine that a Jedi's lightsaber is inseparable from their identity is taught early and reinforced constantly. The phrase is delivered explicitly by Obi-Wan to Anakin in *Attack of the Clones*, after Anakin loses his weapon during the pursuit of Zam Wesell on Coruscant: "This weapon is your life." Anakin's reply, "I've heard that before," underscores that this is recited Order doctrine, not Obi-Wan's invention.

The teaching has practical and symbolic weight. The saber is keyed to the wielder's Force signature, built by their own hands around a crystal they personally bonded with on Ilum (depicted in *The Clone Wars* episode "The Gathering"). To lose it is to lose a piece of one's connection to the Force. Yoda formalizes this in the same episode: the saber is "the weapon of a Jedi Knight, more than that, it is a Jedi's life."

### When the Bond Breaks

A training bond can be severed three ways: death of one partner, the fall of one to the dark side, or deliberate severance.

Death-severance is the most common in the prequel and Imperial eras. Obi-Wan feels Qui-Gon die through the laser gates on Naboo. Cal Kestis feels Jaro Tapal die during their escape from the *Albedo Brave* on Bracca, an event that in *Jedi: Fallen Order* gives him recurring trauma flashbacks for years. Caleb Dume (later Kanan Jarrus) loses Depa Billaba during Order 66 on Kaller in *Star Wars: Kanan* (Marvel comic), and the wound shapes every reluctance he later brings to teaching Ezra.

Falling-severance is its own category. Obi-Wan does not lose Anakin in a clean cut; the bond becomes a wound he carries for the rest of his life, dramatized in the *Obi-Wan Kenobi* series, where Vader's pain still bleeds across the link. Cere Junda's fall is more literal: under Inquisitor torture she gave up the location of her Padawan Trilla Suduri, who was then broken into the Second Sister. Cere afterward severs herself from the Force entirely, unable to face what the bond had become. *Jedi: Fallen Order* and the *Star Wars Jedi: Battle Scars* novel make her arc the canonical case study in survivor's guilt.

Deliberate severance is rare and carries its own cost. The trauma of a forcibly cut bond is depicted as physical: practitioners describe it as an amputation, a phantom limb that aches in the Force.

## The Training Path

### Ranks

Canon recognizes a four-stage progression:

1. **Initiate (Youngling)**: Temple-raised children training in clans, ages roughly four to thirteen.
2. **Padawan**: Apprentice paired one-to-one with a Knight or Master, marked by the braid.
3. **Knight**: Full member of the Order, entitled to operate independently and eventually take a Padawan.
4. **Master**: Granted to a Knight who has demonstrated wisdom and, traditionally, trained a Padawan to Knighthood. Only Masters sit on the High Council, though in *Revenge of the Sith* Anakin is appointed to the Council without the rank, a deliberate provocation by Palpatine.

Above these, the title Grand Master is held by the Order's most senior figure, Yoda in the prequel era and (later, briefly) Luke Skywalker.

### The Trials

To become a Knight, a Padawan must pass the Jedi Trials. The five-trial structure, originally codified in *The Jedi Path* and other Legends sources, has been carried into canon by reference rather than systematic restatement. The trials are:

- **Trial of Skill**: demonstrated mastery of lightsaber combat and Force technique.
- **Trial of Courage**: confronting fear in genuine danger.
- **Trial of the Flesh**: enduring physical pain or loss. Anakin's mechanical right arm, lost to Dooku on Geonosis, is treated by the Council as having satisfied this trial.
- **Trial of the Spirit**: facing one's own inner darkness, often through vision or meditation. Luke's cave on Dagobah in *The Empire Strikes Back* is the archetypal version.
- **Trial of Insight**: piercing deception, perceiving truth.

The trials can be waived in extremity. Obi-Wan is Knighted by the Council immediately after Naboo, his combat against Maul accepted as proof. Caleb Dume never completes them; Order 66 ends his Padawanship. Ahsoka leaves the Order before formal Knighthood, returning her braid to Anakin in the *Clone Wars* episode "The Wrong Jedi."

### Building One's Lightsaber

The construction of a lightsaber is the milestone that announces a Padawan is a serious instrument of the Order. The Gathering on Ilum, depicted across the *Clone Wars* arc beginning with "The Gathering" (S5E6), sends Initiates into the crystal caves to find the kyber that will choose them. They then assemble the weapon under guidance, often with the help of architect droid Huyang aboard the *Crucible* (the same droid who later trains Sabine on Ahsoka's ship). Huyang has overseen lightsaber construction for "a thousand generations" of Jedi, per his dialogue in both *Clone Wars* and *Ahsoka*.

### Forms of Practice

Daily training combines:

- **Katas**: choreographed lightsaber sequences drilled to the point of reflex, the foundation of all seven combat Forms.
- **Sparring**: paired combat against peers and instructors, often blindfolded with stun-bolt remotes (the technique Obi-Wan teaches Luke aboard the *Falcon* in *A New Hope*).
- **Meditation**: solo and shared, used to deepen Force connection and process emotion.
- **Scholarly study**: Archives work, holocron consultation, history, philosophy, xenology, diplomacy, language.
- **Field assignments**: the Padawan accompanies the Master on real Council assignments, where every encounter is also a lesson.

### Failure and Recovery

Failure is built into the path. Padawans are expected to fail and to be corrected without humiliation. Anakin's recurring failures with attachment, Ahsoka's wartime mistakes, Ezra's overreaches with the Sith holocron in *Rebels*: each is treated as material to be worked, not grounds for expulsion. The serious failure modes are the ones the Master cannot see in time, and the cases where pride prevents the apprentice from naming what went wrong.

## Notable Lineages

The longest traceable canonical Jedi lineage runs:

**Yoda → Dooku → Qui-Gon Jinn → Obi-Wan Kenobi → Anakin Skywalker → Ahsoka Tano → Sabine Wren**

Yoda trained Dooku (stated in *Attack of the Clones*, expanded in *Tales of the Jedi* on Disney+). Dooku trained Qui-Gon (depicted in *Tales of the Jedi* and *Master & Apprentice* by Claudia Gray). Qui-Gon trained Obi-Wan (*The Phantom Menace*). Obi-Wan trained Anakin (the prequel trilogy). Anakin trained Ahsoka (*The Clone Wars*). Ahsoka took Sabine in the post-Imperial era (*Ahsoka*, Disney+, 2023). Seven generations, one unbroken chain, every link confirmed in canon screen or novel material.

A parallel branch: Yoda also instructed Mace Windu in some canonical accounts, though Mace's specific lineage is less detailed; Mace in turn trained Depa Billaba, who trained Caleb Dume / Kanan Jarrus, who trained Ezra Bridger. Three of those four died violently before completing the next handoff.

Post-purge lineages are shorter and more improvised. **Obi-Wan trained Luke Skywalker** in the brief windows of *A New Hope* and *Obi-Wan Kenobi*. **Yoda trained Luke** on Dagobah. Luke then attempted to rebuild the Order, with **Ben Solo** as his most promising student until the events at the temple shown in fragments across *The Last Jedi*. After the temple's destruction Luke trained **Rey** on Ahch-To, and briefly **Grogu**, who chose to leave with Din Djarin in *The Book of Boba Fett* rather than complete the path.

The Sith maintain the inverse structure under the Rule of Two, established by Darth Bane and quoted by Yoda in *Attack of the Clones*: "Always two there are, no more, no less. A master and an apprentice." The canonical chain visible on screen is **Plagueis → Sidious → Maul / Tyranus / Vader**, with Maul, Dooku, and Anakin each trained as apprentices by Palpatine in succession (Maul demoted rather than killed when presumed dead, Dooku murdered to make room for Anakin, Anakin reduced to figurehead after the Emperor finds Snoke and Rey as long-term projects).

Other notable canonical pairings include **Shaak Ti → Cal Kestis** (Cal trained as an Initiate under Shaak Ti at the Temple before being assigned to **Jaro Tapal** as Padawan, per *Jedi: Fallen Order*'s backstory) and **Cere Junda → Trilla Suduri**, the broken bond that drives both *Fallen Order* and *Jedi: Survivor*.

## Transmission of Knowledge

### Holocrons

Holocrons are crystalline data devices, cube-shaped for Jedi and pyramidal for Sith, that store recorded teachings accessible only through the Force. They function as a Master's voice preserved past their death. The Jedi Temple Archives held thousands; a small number survived Order 66 in the hands of Jocasta Nu (depicted in the *Darth Vader: Dark Lord of the Sith* comic) and others. Ezra Bridger opens a Jedi holocron in *Rebels* season 1 and a Sith holocron in season 2; the contrast between the two is itself a lesson.

### Verbal and Embodied Transmission

Most Jedi teaching is not written. Forms are taught hand-to-hand, philosophy is transmitted in spoken instruction during meditation or after combat, judgment is conveyed by example. This is why the Order is so vulnerable to a single generational rupture: kill the Masters and the technique starts to vanish within a generation.

### The Sacred Texts

Luke retrieves the original Jedi texts (the *Aionomica* and *Rammahgon* in supporting canon material) from the first Jedi Temple on Ahch-To. In *The Last Jedi*, Yoda calls down lightning to burn the tree library, telling Luke that what Rey needs she already carries. Rey has secretly removed the books beforehand; they appear in the *Millennium Falcon* in *The Rise of Skywalker*.

### The Cost of a Broken Chain

Order 66 did not just kill Jedi, it killed the next generation's teachers. By the time Luke begins his self-directed reconstruction, the Order's living memory is reduced to two old men (Yoda, Obi-Wan), a handful of fugitives (Ahsoka, Kanan, Cal, Cere, others), and the texts. Luke has to assemble a working understanding of Jedi practice from fragments. His later failure with Ben Solo is in part a failure of the broken chain: he had no Master to consult on the warning signs of a falling apprentice.

## Mentorship Across the Grey

Several canonical mentorships happen outside the Order's traditional structure, and canon treats most of them as legitimate even when irregular.

**Qui-Gon Jinn** is the prototype of the maverick Master. *Master & Apprentice* (Claudia Gray) makes explicit that Qui-Gon repeatedly declines a seat on the Council because his commitment to the Living Force conflicts with the Council's institutional caution. He insists on training Anakin against the Council's explicit refusal, and his deathbed extraction of Obi-Wan's promise binds the Order to a decision it had rejected.

**Ahsoka Tano** trains Sabine Wren without herself being a Jedi. Ahsoka left the Order in *The Clone Wars* and has refused the title since; her own teacher Huyang remarks in *Ahsoka* that Sabine is the least Force-talented student he has seen in a thousand generations. Ahsoka takes her anyway, and the bond they build is real in the Force regardless of institutional sanction.

**Kanan Jarrus** is the canonical reluctant Master. In *Rebels*, he is a Padawan-rank survivor with no Trials, no Knighthood, and active trauma from Order 66. He takes Ezra Bridger because no one else will. He invents the curriculum as he goes, consults Yoda's voice through a holocron, and eventually undergoes a personal Knighting ceremony with the Grand Inquisitor on Stygeon Prime as the cost. Ezra in turn is largely self-taught: he builds his own lightsaber, a hybrid blue saber with an integrated stun blaster, reflecting both his street-survivor pragmatism and the absence of an orthodox tradition to constrain him.

**Cal Kestis** trains under **Cere Junda** in *Fallen Order* and *Jedi: Survivor*, but Cere is not his Master in any institutional sense. She is a fellow survivor working back toward the Force after years of severance. The teaching that passes between them is mutual: Cal learns from Cere's recovered knowledge, Cere is led back to the Force partly by Cal's persistence. By *Survivor*, Cal is teaching others in turn, including Merrin's Force-adjacent Nightsister magick traditions running alongside his Jedi training.

The pattern in all of these: when the Order is intact, deviation from the Master-Padawan structure is rare and contested. When the Order is gone, the structure is whatever two people can build between them. The bond, the transmission, the doctrine that the saber is your life, all of it survives because someone insisted on teaching someone else, in the absence of any institution telling them they were allowed to.
